# Parent-project implications

## Inherited evidence

The standalone predecessor established a narrow one-event result: small oldest-first demotion of exact-backed result bodies preserved two historical read decisions and admitted observations that had previously been capacity-censored.

## Standalone observation

This experiment reused the same rule at successive authentic result-delivery boundaries. Across two trajectories, four of four authorized post-call pressure events were restored to feasibility under the unchanged 25,088-token envelope and 4,096-token reserve. Exact receipts were behaviorally usable: the model requested four exact reopens of demoted objects.

No candidate changed. Both trajectories continued acquiring or reopening information for all three calls, and neither submitted. One target read duplicated still-resident material; the other extended into adjacent unread lines.

## Claim suitable for later consideration

A conservatively scoped claim that could be considered for later import is:

> At two continuation trajectories on one task/world, pressure-triggered minimum-necessary demotion of older exact-backed results repeatedly admitted newly requested exact results while preserving a fixed response reserve. Model-initiated exact fault-in worked, but the bounded continuation produced no artifact action.

This does not validate a general context manager, semantic eviction, a universal recency rule, or long-run working-set stability.

## Unresolved

It remains unknown whether the policy can recur safely beyond two post-call reductions per trajectory, whether reopen behavior eventually converges or thrashes, whether the additional reads were necessary, and whether the recurrent prefix-rewrite cost can be reduced without losing the capacity effect.
